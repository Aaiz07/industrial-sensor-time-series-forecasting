============================================================
FINAL INTERPRETATION
============================================================

1. Temperature
Persistence is the strongest forecasting strategy for temperature at the 60-second horizon.

2. Magnetic sensors
Ridge provides substantially lower RMSE than persistence, but the walk-forward R2 remains close to zero. Therefore the magnetic channels have limited predictable pointwise variation at this horizon.

3. Vibration sensors
XGBoost has the lowest average RMSE among the tested strategies, but its walk-forward R2 remains strongly negative. Therefore vibration forecasting should not be described as reliable point prediction under the current data and evaluation setup.

4. Generalization
The difference between training performance and walk-forward performance demonstrates that temporal distribution shift is a major challenge in this dataset.

5. Model complexity
Increasing model complexity did not consistently solve the forecasting problem. The persistence baseline remains strong for slow-moving temperature data.

6. Overall conclusion
The project successfully established a leakage-controlled 60-second forecasting pipeline and evaluated model generalization across multiple chronological windows.

IMPORTANT:
The Step 21 training metrics must not be presented as unbiased forecasting accuracy. The Step 20 walk-forward results are the primary evidence of generalization.
